## Script formula (from the transcripts)
1. **Hook, sentence 1.** A man does something absurd, often with a deadline, in 12-18 words.
   - "He cut grooves into four brand new racing tires the night before the biggest race of the year."
   - "He threw a javelin so far that the judges at the far end ran for their lives."
   - "He jumped over the high bar backwards, and everyone laughed."
   - Flop hook (gym horse): "Every day, prisoners vaulted a gym horse in the middle of the camp yard." It has no single hero, no absurd act and no stakes.
2. **Mockery in short spoken lines.** For example "You just cut away our grip!", "looked like a fish flopping off a boat", and "the cheese grater".
3. **Stakes made concrete.** "the winner's tires go on every F1 car in the world", or "tires you start on are the tires you finish on".
4. **The hero looks wrong, at length.** For example "By lap 50, it's a whole lap behind." The audience is let to believe he lost.
5. **A quiet tell.** "He's not even watching the race. He's watching the clouds."
6. **The payoff is SHOWN.** The mechanism is told in ONE analogy line inside the action: "smooth tires... ride on top of it like a wet glass sliding on a table". The rival fails visibly; the leader crashes into hay bales.
7. **The ending.**
   - The 1.5M hit ends on the payoff image. There is NO "in real life" history outro and NO Part 2.
   - The 100-105s videos add a "You see, in real life, in [year], [real name]..." outro of about 20s. Some of them still hit 437-478K.
8. **Pace.** About 4 words/s (the tyre video has 265 words in 67s; the long ones about 3.5 words/s).

- Shot types:

  - big prop close-ups (tyre on a grinder, a cut groove)
  - wide track shots
  - a low tyre close-up in rain
  - a cockpit POV
  - a crowd shot
- Weather drives the story: dry, then sunny forecast, then drops, then a downpour.
- The colours stay saturated even in the rain.

## What to copy
- Use a single hero with an absurd act plus a deadline in sentence 1.
- Spend most of the runtime on the hero looking wrong.
- Put the one-line analogy inside the payoff action, and show the payoff.
- Use a standalone complete story at 60-67s. Our mid-sentence Part 1 cut is a different, unproven bet on this format.
- Use in-world signs for text.
- Make the caption the title only, with no hashtags.
